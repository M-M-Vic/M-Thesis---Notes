\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

This section collects the background material used throughout the derivations, so that the later arguments can stay focused on their primary objectives.

The central object throughout is the (P)PGF of the model in question, together with the system's idle probability for some corollaries.


\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$M/M/1$}{M/M/1} queue}\label{ssec:mm1}

The canonical $M/M/1$ queue is the foundation of queueing theory and is treated in many textbooks; we follow \cite{adan2002queueing}.
Customers arrive according to a Poisson process ---i.e. the inter-arrival times are exponentially distributed with parameter $\lambda$---, service times are exponentially distributed with parameter $\mu$, and a single server operates in first-come-first-serve (FCFS) ---also known as first-in-first-out (FIFO)--- order. The system is stable if and only if the load $\rho = \lambda/\mu < 1$.

Let $\pi_n$ be the limiting probability of finding $n$ customers in the system. Under stability, the detailed balance equations $\lambda\,\pi_n = \mu\,\pi_{n+1}$ ($n \geq 0$) together with the normalisation $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\pi_n=1$ give us the geometric steady-state distribution of the number of customers in the system:
\begin{equation*}
    \pi_n = (1-\rho)\,\rho^n, \quad n \ge 0. 
\end{equation*}
By the PASTA property (\S\ref{ssec:priority}), this also corresponds to the distribution seen by arriving customers. Let $L$ be the number of customers in the system, including any in service. Its expectation is
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}[L] = \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}.
\end{equation*}
From this, the expected sojourn time $W$---the time between a customer's arrival and their departure after service---follows by Little's Law ($\mathbb{E}[L] = \lambda\mathbb{E}[W]$):
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb{E}[W] = \frac{\mathbb{E}[L]}{\lambda} = \frac{1/\mu}{1-\rho}.
\end{equation*}

A less common but highly relevant concept is the Busy Period ($BP$): the time between a customer arriving to an idle system and the departure that next leaves it empty. Its Laplace--Stieltjes Transform (LST) and expectation are known:
\begin{align}
    \widetilde{BP}(s) &= \frac{(\mu+\lambda+s) - \sqrt{(\mu+\lambda+s)^2 - 4\mu\lambda}}{2\lambda},\label{eq:mm1:bp_lst} \\
    \mathbb{E}[BP]    &= \frac{1}{\mu(1-\rho)}. \label{eq:mm1:bp_mean}
\end{align}

\subsection{Non-preemptive priority and the PASTA property}\label{ssec:priority}

Throughout this thesis the two customer classes are served under a \emph{non-preemptive}
priority discipline: whenever the server becomes free it admits a waiting
class-1 customer if any are present, and a class-2 customer only otherwise, but a customer
already in service is \emph{never} interrupted---a class-1 arrival that finds a class-2
customer in service waits until that service completes. Priority therefore governs only the
order in which the queues are drained, not the service in progress. Because the service rate
$\mu$ is common to both classes, the \emph{memorylessness} of the exponential service time
makes the residual service of the customer in progress $\mathrm{Exp}(\mu)$ regardless of its
class; hence the class of the in-service customer does not influence the future evolution of
the queue lengths, which is why a single in-service customer of either class suffices to
mark the server busy.

We also invoke repeatedly the \emph{PASTA} property (\emph{Poisson Arrivals See Time
Averages})~\cite{adan2002queueing}: if a system is fed by a Poisson arrival process that
satisfies the \emph{lack-of-anticipation assumption} (at each instant the future increments
of the arrival process are independent of the current system state), then the long-run
fraction of arrivals that find the system in a given state equals the stationary probability
of that state, so the distribution seen by an arriving customer coincides with the
time-stationary one. Both hypotheses hold here: the exogenous class-$i$ arrivals are
independent Poisson processes at rates $\lambda_i$, and they are independent of the system's
internal exponential service, jockeying and abandonment clocks, so they cannot anticipate
the future. By Poisson \emph{superposition} the merged arrival stream is itself Poisson at
rate $\lambda=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$, so PASTA applies to the aggregate state; and since each
class stream is an independent Poisson process---equivalently, an i.i.d.\
$\lambda_i/\lambda$ \emph{thinning} of the merged stream---PASTA applies to each class
separately.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$M/M/1{+}M$}{M/M/1+M} queue (Erlang-A)}\label{ssec:erlangA}

Another model relevant to this thesis is the $M/M/1+M$ queue, also known as the \emph{Erlang-A} model. It extends the $M/M/1$ queue ---Poisson arrivals at rate $\lambda$, exponential service at rate $\mu$--- with customer impatience: each \emph{waiting} customer (the one in service is not impatient) independently abandons after an exponentially distributed patience time with rate $\theta$. Crucially, unlike the $M/M/1$ it does not require $\rho=\lambda/\mu<1$ for stability: it is positive recurrent for all $\lambda, \mu, \theta > 0$.

The steady-state distribution follows from its balance equations, where $n$ denotes the number of customers in the \emph{system}:
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda\,\pi_n = (\mu + n\theta)\,\pi_{n+1}, \qquad n \ge 0.
\end{equation*}
Iterating yields
\begin{equation*}
    \pi_n = \pi_0\,\frac{(\lambda/\theta)^n}{(\mu/\theta)^{(n)}} \quad n \ge 0;
    \quad \text{where} \quad a^{(n)} = a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
\end{equation*}
Normalisation gives the non-trivial idle probability $\pi_0 = [{}_1F_1(1;\,\mu/\theta;\,\lambda/\theta)]^{-1}$ (see \S\ref{ssec:kummer} for the Kummer function ${}_1F_1$), and summing the geometric-like terms shows that the queue-length PGF is correspondingly a \emph{ratio} of Kummer functions, $\Pi(z) = {}_1F_1(1;\,\mu/\theta;\,(\lambda/\theta)z)\,/\,{}_1F_1(1;\,\mu/\theta;\,\lambda/\theta)$. The same closed form is derived by Kapodistria~\cite[Thm.~3]{kapodistria2011synchronized} as the no-synchronisation limit of a queue with synchronised abandonments, there in the sojourn-time-impatience convention (the customer in service also abandons), which amounts to replacing $\mu$ by $\mu+\theta$.

Unlike the textbook facts collected above, the busy-period identity that follows is the one preliminary we derive from scratch, as Model-$C_2$ rests on it directly. The \emph{Busy Period} of this $M/M/1+M$ is far more complex than its $M/M/1$ counterpart. We record it for the class-1 subsystem used in Model-$C_2$, with arrival rate $\lambda_1$, service rate $\mu$ and abandonment rate $\theta_1$, so that the relevant idle probability is $\pi_0=[{}_1F_1(1;\,\mu/\theta_1;\,\lambda_1/\theta_1)]^{-1}$. Writing $B_C$ for this busy period, its LST and expectation are
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
    \widetilde{B}_C(s) = \cfrac{\mu}{\lambda_1+\mu+s
      -\cfrac{\lambda_1(\mu+\theta_1)}{\lambda_1+\mu+\theta_1+s
      -\cfrac{\lambda_1(\mu+2\theta_1)}{\lambda_1+\mu+2\theta_1+s-\dotsb}}},
    \label{eq:erlangA:bp_lst}
    \end{split} \\
    \begin{split}
        \mathbb{E}[B_C] = \frac{1}{\lambda_1}\Bigl({}_1F_1\bigl(1;\,\mu/\theta_1;\,\lambda_1/\theta_1\bigr) - 1\Bigr)
        \label{eq:erlangA:bp_mean}
    \end{split}
\end{align}
The continued fraction~\eqref{eq:erlangA:bp_lst} is generated by a first-passage recursion.
Let $\phi_n(s)$ denote the LST of the first-passage (sub-busy-period) time from $n$
customers in the system down to $n-1$. Conditioning on the first transition out of state
$n$---an arrival (rate $\lambda_1$) to state $n+1$, or a departure by service completion or
abandonment (combined rate $\mu+(n-1)\theta_1$: one customer in service plus $n-1$ waiting,
each impatient at rate $\theta_1$) to state $n-1$---the strong Markov property gives
\begin{equation}
    \phi_n(s) \;=\; \frac{\mu+(n-1)\theta_1}
        {\lambda_1+\mu+(n-1)\theta_1+s-\lambda_1\,\phi_{n+1}(s)},
    \qquad n\ge 1, \qquad \widetilde{B}_C(s)=\phi_1(s).
    \label{eq:erlangA:r_recursion}
\end{equation}
Unrolling~\eqref{eq:erlangA:r_recursion} from $n=1$ reproduces the continued
fraction~\eqref{eq:erlangA:bp_lst} term by term. The continued fraction also sums in
closed form, and the argument is short enough to give in full. Fix $s>0$ and scale the
rates by $\theta_1$, setting
\begin{equation}
    a=\frac{\mu}{\theta_1}, \qquad b=\frac{s}{\theta_1}, \qquad c=\frac{\lambda_1}{\theta_1},
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
so that~\eqref{eq:erlangA:r_recursion} reads
$\phi_n(s)=(a+n-1)\big/\bigl((a+n-1)+b+c-c\,\phi_{n+1}(s)\bigr)$. Consider the integrals
\begin{equation*}
    I(p) \;=\; \int_0^1 t^{\,p}\,(1-t)^{\,b-1}\,e^{-ct}\,dt,
    \qquad p>-1,
\end{equation*}
which by the Euler representation~\eqref{eq:kummer:euler} are Beta-function multiples of
Kummer values, $I(p)=B(p+1,\,b)\,{}_1F_1(p+1;\,p+1+b;\,-c)$. Integrating the exact
derivative $\frac{d}{dt}\bigl[t^{\,p}(1-t)^{\,b}e^{-ct}\bigr]$ over $[0,1]$ --- the
boundary terms vanish for $p,b>0$ --- and splitting
$(1-t)^{b}=(1-t)^{b-1}-t\,(1-t)^{b-1}$ in the two terms where it appears yields the
three-term recurrence
\begin{equation}
    (p+b+c)\,I(p) \;=\; p\,I(p-1) \;+\; c\,I(p+1),
    \label{eq:erlangA:threeterm}
\end{equation}
a relation of contiguous type for ${}_1F_1$ (obtainable equivalently by combining the
contiguous relations of~\cite[\S13.3]{dlmf}). Dividing~\eqref{eq:erlangA:threeterm} at
$p=a+n-1$ by $I(a+n-2)$ shows that the ratios $R_n:=I(a+n-1)/I(a+n-2)$ satisfy exactly
the recursion of $\phi_n$. Two further facts pin the identification. First, by
Laplace's method at the endpoint $t=1$, $I(p)\sim e^{-c}\,\Gamma(b)\,p^{-b}$ as
$p\to\infty$: the solution $\{I(p)\}$ decays only polynomially, whereas the dominant
solution of~\eqref{eq:erlangA:threeterm} grows superexponentially (its consecutive
ratio grows like $p/c$), so $\{I(p)\}$ is the \emph{minimal (recessive)} solution.
Second, by \emph{Pincherle's theorem} the convergent continued fraction equals
precisely the ratio built from the minimal solution. Hence
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{B}_C(s)\;=\;\phi_1(s)\;=\;\frac{I(a)}{I(a-1)}
    \;=\;\frac{B(a+1,\,b)\;{}_1F_1\bigl(a+1;\,a+1+b;\,-c\bigr)}
              {B(a,\,b)\;{}_1F_1\bigl(a;\,a+b;\,-c\bigr)},
    \label{eq:erlangA:cf_kummer}
\end{equation}
a contiguous ratio of the form~\eqref{eq:kummer:ratio}. This identity is used, with
$s=\lambda_2(1-y)$, in the boundedness route for Model-$C_2$ (\S\ref{sec:model_c2},
Equation~\eqref{eq:C2:Bc_Kummer}).

\subsection{Pollaczek--Khinchine formula}\label{ssec:pk}

The $M/G/1$ queue~\cite{adan2002queueing} is a single-server queue with Poisson arrivals
at rate $\lambda$ and generally distributed i.i.d.\ service times $B$ with LST $\widetilde{B}(s) = \mathbb{E}[e^{-sB}]$.
For stability purposes, the system load $\rho_B = \lambda\,\mathbb{E}[B] < 1$ is required
(written $\rho_B$ to keep the symbol $\rho$ for the two-class load of this thesis).
Under these two conditions ---Poisson arrivals and stability--- the Pollaczek--Khinchine (PK) formula
gives the PGF of the stationary number of customers in the system:
\begin{equation}
    L(z) \;=\; \frac{(1-\rho_B)(1-z)\,\widetilde{B}(\lambda(1-z))}{\widetilde{B}(\lambda(1-z)) - z}.
    \label{eq:pk:pgf}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Confluent hypergeometric function}\label{ssec:kummer}

The \emph{Kummer confluent hypergeometric function of the first kind} appears in several derivations in this thesis; it reads as follows:
\begin{equation}
    {}_1F_1(a;\,b;\,z) \;=\;
        \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{a^{(n)}}{b^{(n)}\,n!}\,z^n,
    \qquad a^{(n)} = \prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(a+k),\quad a^{(0)}=1,
    \label{eq:kummer:series}
\end{equation}
where $b$ is not $0$ or a negative integer ---i.e. $b \notin \{0,-1,-2,\dots\}$--- and the series converges for all
$z\in\mathbb{C}$.
The function ${}_1F_1(a;\,b;\,z)$ is the unique solution of \emph{Kummer's equation}~\cite{dlmf}:
\begin{equation*}
    z\,u'' + (b - z)\,u' - a\,u = 0,
\end{equation*}
normalised by ${}_1F_1(a;\,b;\,0) = 1$.

For this thesis, two identities related to it are used.
First, the \emph{Euler integral representation}, for $a,b>0$ we have:
\begin{equation}
    {}_1F_1(a;\,a+b;\,z) \;=\; \frac{1}{B(a,b)} \int_0^1 t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1} e^{zt}\,dt, 
    \label{eq:kummer:euler}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the \emph{Beta function} $B(a,b)=\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)/\Gamma(a+b)$, and $\Gamma$ is the \emph{Gamma function}.
Secondly, \emph{contiguous ratios}: ratios of two ${}_1F_1$ values whose parameters
differ by integer shifts. Two patterns arise in this thesis. The \emph{simultaneous
shift}
\begin{equation}
    \frac{{}_1F_1(a+1;\,a+1+b;\,z)}{{}_1F_1(a;\,a+b;\,z)},
    \label{eq:kummer:ratio}
\end{equation}
in which both parameters move by one so that their difference $b$ is preserved, is by
the Euler representation~\eqref{eq:kummer:euler} a ratio of two weighted Beta integrals
differing only in the exponent of $t$; it appears as the closed-form value of the
Erlang-A busy-period continued fraction (\S\ref{ssec:erlangA},
Equation~\eqref{eq:erlangA:cf_kummer}) and hence in the boundary function of
Model-$C_2$. The \emph{first-parameter shift}
${}_1F_1(a+1;\,b;\,z)\big/{}_1F_1(a;\,b;\,z)$, with the second parameter fixed, appears
instead in the boundary function of Model-$B_2$ (\S\ref{sec:model_b2}).

\subsection{First-order linear ODEs and PDEs}\label{ssec:ode}

Since jockeying and abandonment add state-dependent rates to the balance equations, derivative terms appear in the derivations, requiring us to solve first-order linear ODEs and PDEs for the PGF. The three methods below are invoked at specific steps of the analysis.

\subsubsection{Integrating factor}\label{sssec:if}

This method is standard to solve ---for each fixed $y$--- a \emph{first-order linear ODE in $x$}. Such an equation has standard form
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dP}{dx} + p(x;\,y)\,P \;=\; q(x;\,y). \label{eq:ode:standard}
\end{equation}
The \emph{integrating factor} $\mu_I(x;y) = \exp\!\bigl(\int_0^x p(t;y)\,dt\bigr)$ converts~\eqref{eq:ode:standard} to $\frac{d}{dx}[P\cdot\mu_I] = q\cdot\mu_I$. Integrating from $0$ to $x$ gives the general solution
\begin{equation}
    P(x,y) \;=\; \frac{1}{\mu_I(x;\,y)}
        \left[C_0(y) + \int_0^x q(t;\,y)\,\mu_I(t;\,y)\,dt\right],
    \label{eq:ode:general}
\end{equation}
where $C_0(y)$ is an arbitrary function of $y$, determined by a boundary condition.

\subsubsection{Method of Characteristics}\label{sssec:moc}

This method is standard for solving a \emph{first-order linear PDE in both $x$ and $y$} simultaneously. It reduces the PDE to a family of ODEs along the so-called \emph{characteristic curves} in the $(x,y)$-plane; along each curve, the PDE collapses to a single-variable ODE.

A PDE of the form
\begin{equation}
    a \frac{\partial P}{\partial x} + b \frac{\partial P}{\partial y} = cP + d
    \label{eq:ode:pde}
\end{equation}
is solved by tracing the characteristic curve $(x(t),y(t))$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dx}{dt} = a, \qquad \frac{dy}{dt} = b.
    \label{eq:ode:char}
\end{equation}
By the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dt}P(x(t),y(t)) = a\,P_x + b\,P_y$, so~\eqref{eq:ode:pde} reduces to
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dP}{dt} = cP + d,
    \label{eq:ode:charode}
\end{equation}
a first-order linear ODE in $t$. Its homogeneous part $\frac{dP}{dt}=cP$ is solved at once by separation of variables; the inhomogeneous term $d$ is then absorbed either by the integrating factor (\S\ref{sssec:if}) or, once that homogeneous solution is in hand, by variation of parameters (\S\ref{sssec:vop}). When $a$ and $b$ are constants, the characteristics are straight lines and the quantity
\begin{equation*}
    \xi \;:=\; b\,x - a\,y
\end{equation*}
is preserved along each curve ---it is the \emph{first integral} that labels each characteristic. The integration constant of~\eqref{eq:ode:charode} therefore varies between characteristics: it is an arbitrary function $F(\xi)$. Re-expressing the solution in the original variables $(x,y)$ via~\eqref{eq:ode:char} gives the general solution of~\eqref{eq:ode:pde}. In the PGF setting, $F$ is identified with a boundary generating function, and analyticity of $P(x,y)$ for $|x|,|y|\leq 1$ uniquely determines it.


\subsubsection{Variation of parameters}\label{sssec:vop}

The integrating factor of \S\ref{sssec:if} builds a solution from scratch by constructing $\mu_I$. Frequently, however, we \emph{already} hold a nonzero solution of the homogeneous equation ---most importantly, the one the Method of Characteristics (\S\ref{sssec:moc}) produces along each characteristic curve. \emph{Variation of parameters} is the technique that turns any such known homogeneous solution directly into the general solution of the inhomogeneous equation, with no further integrating factor required. We describe it in the form in which it is used later.

Consider a first-order linear ODE written as
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dP}{dx} = f(x)\,P + h(x),
    \label{eq:ode:vop_standard}
\end{equation}
and suppose a nonzero homogeneous solution $P_h$ is known, that is, $\frac{dP_h}{dx} = f(x)\,P_h$. The homogeneous equation then has general solution $C\,P_h(x)$ for an arbitrary constant $C$. To solve the full equation~\eqref{eq:ode:vop_standard}, we \emph{vary the constant}: we promote $C$ to a function $C(x)$ and look for a solution of the form
\begin{equation*}
    P(x) = C(x)\,P_h(x).
\end{equation*}
Differentiating by the product rule and using $P_h' = f\,P_h$,
\begin{align*}
    \begin{split}
        \frac{dP}{dx} &= C'(x)\,P_h(x) + C(x)\,P_h'(x)
    \end{split}\\
    \begin{split}
        &= C'(x)\,P_h(x) + f(x)\,\bigl[C(x)\,P_h(x)\bigr]
         = C'(x)\,P_h(x) + f(x)\,P(x).
    \end{split}
\end{align*}
Substituting this back into~\eqref{eq:ode:vop_standard}, the common term $f(x)\,P(x)$ cancels from both sides and only the derivative of the varied constant survives:
\begin{equation*}
    C'(x)\,P_h(x) = h(x)
    \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
    C'(x) = \frac{h(x)}{P_h(x)}.
\end{equation*}
Integrating from a base point $x_0$ gives $C(x) = F + \int_{x_0}^{x} h(t)/P_h(t)\,dt$ with $F$ an arbitrary constant, and hence the general solution
\begin{equation}
    P(x) = P_h(x)\left[\,F + \int_{x_0}^{x} \frac{h(t)}{P_h(t)}\,dt\,\right].
    \label{eq:ode:vop_general}
\end{equation}
The known homogeneous solution $P_h$ thus does double duty: it is the overall prefactor and, as $1/P_h$, the weight inside the integral. The constant $F$ is fixed by a boundary condition. Taking in particular $P_h = 1/\mu_I$ reproduces the integrating-factor formula~\eqref{eq:ode:general} of \S\ref{sssec:if}.

This is exactly the step that completes the Method of Characteristics for an \emph{inhomogeneous} PDE. Once \S\ref{sssec:moc} has reduced the PDE to an ODE of the form~\eqref{eq:ode:vop_standard} along a characteristic and supplied its homogeneous solution $P_h$, formula~\eqref{eq:ode:vop_general} delivers the full solution along that characteristic, the constant $F$ becoming an arbitrary function $F(\xi)$ of the characteristic label. Model-$B$ (\S\ref{sec:model_b}) is solved by precisely this pairing.
