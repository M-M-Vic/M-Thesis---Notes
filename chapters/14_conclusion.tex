\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis analysed a two-class non-preemptive priority $M/M/1$ queue under two waiting-room mechanisms --- jockeying and abandonment --- and obtained the exact stationary bivariate PGF $P(x,y)$ in closed or integral form for five models: the baseline $A$, one-way jockeying $B_2$, class-1 abandonment $C_2$, and the head-of-line variants $\BH$ and $\CH$, with the empty-state probabilities $\pi_0$ and $\pi(0,0)$ pulled into a corollary in each case. Underpinning these is a single structural finding: a single object governs tractability throughout --- the algebraic form of the fundamental PGF equation of Lemma~\ref{lem:gen:fundamental_equation}. What the model hierarchy ultimately delivers is therefore less a catalogue of closed forms than a map of \emph{why} each model is solvable or not, and it is that map, rather than any individual transform, that we regard as the central contribution.

\subsection{The obstruction taxonomy}

The remark following Lemma~\ref{lem:gen:fundamental_equation} reads the fundamental equation as a competition between an algebraic coefficient and two convection terms, and the analysis bears out a clean dichotomy, summarised model by model in the comparison of \S\ref{sec:comparison} (Table~\ref{tab:comparison}). Where the equation is solvable, the unknown boundary function is determined by exactly one of four \emph{pinning mechanisms}. The first is evaluation at a kernel root lying strictly inside the unit disc, the purely algebraic route of the baseline Model-$A$ (Section~\ref{sec:model_a}). The second is interior analyticity: when one-way jockeying survives, the factor $(x-y)$ places the operative singularity at the diagonal point $x=y$ inside the disc, and the boundary function is fixed by demanding holomorphy there (Model-$B_2$, Section~\ref{sec:model_b2}). The third is boundary boundedness: when class-1 abandonment survives, the factor $(x-1)$ moves the singularity onto the unit-circle boundary $x=1$, where mere boundedness of the PGF suffices to pin the unknown (Model-$C_2$, Section~\ref{sec:model_c2}). The fourth is head-of-line algebraic collapse: restricting a mechanism to the customer at the head of the queue replaces a length-proportional rate by a constant one, annihilates the derivative term, and returns the equation to the algebraic Model-$A$ form, solvable by the same kernel argument (Models~$\BH$ and~$\CH$, Sections~\ref{sec:model_b21}--\ref{sec:model_c21}).

Where the equation is not solvable, exactly two irreducible obstructions appear, and they are what Models~$X$ and~$B$ exist to expose. The first is \emph{transcendental characteristics}: in the full model the method of characteristics no longer follows straight lines but integral curves of a nonlinear ODE, so the kernel root is unavailable in closed form. The second is \emph{diagonal gradient coupling}: closing the reduced equation requires a Volterra integral equation whose forcing depends on a boundary or diagonal trace of the very solution being sought --- in Model-$B$ the straight-line characteristics survive but the boundary function still satisfies a first-kind Volterra equation, while in Model-$X$ both obstructions compound, the anchoring diagonal value $P(z,z)$ itself becoming unknown. The hierarchy is therefore not a loose collection of cases but a complete enumeration: four ways the functional equation can close, and two reasons it cannot.

\subsection{The mechanism dichotomy}

The two mechanisms act in opposite registers, and the numerics of \S\ref{sec:comparison} make the contrast quantitative. Jockeying \emph{redistributes} delay at fixed total work: relocating a waiting customer conserves the total count, so $\mathbb{E}[N]$, $\pi_0$ and $\pi(0,0)$ are identical to the baseline for every $\gamma_1$, and the mechanism's entire effect is to move delay between classes --- the priority premium $\mathbb{E}[W_2]/\mathbb{E}[W_1]$ inflating to $\premiumBtwoNinety$ at $\rho=0.90$ against the Model-$A$ value $\premiumANinety$.

Abandonment instead \emph{sheds} load: the idle probability rises, every queue shortens, and total work falls --- but at the price of a class-1 loss fraction of $\lossCtwoSeventy$ at $\rho=0.70$, $\theta_1=0.5$, roughly one priority arrival in four lost before service.

This price compels the thesis's sharpest applied caveat: the apparent waiting-time improvement under abandonment is partly attrition, not faster service. The count-based priority wait falls from $\mathbb{E}[W_1]=\uncondWoneA$ in Model-$A$ to $\uncondWoneCtwo$ in Model-$C_2$, yet the conditional mean time-to-service of the $\fracServedCtwo$ of class-1 customers who are actually served is $\condWoneCtwo$; the difference between the count-based figure and the served-customer figure is exactly the queue residence logged by the customers who abandon, so a manager comparing models on $\mathbb{E}[W_1]$ alone would read loss as speed.

\subsection{Operational reading and the deterioration-direction caveat}

These results close the healthcare loop opened in the Introduction. The rates $\gamma_1$ and $\theta_1$ are the model's two clinical dials: $\theta_1$ is the rate at which a waiting high-priority patient leaves the list by death, transfer, or deterioration past the point of benefit, and $\gamma_1$ is the rate at which a waiting class-1 patient is moved into the class-2 queue. Here an honest caveat is owed. The Introduction motivates jockeying as \emph{deterioration} --- a waiting patient becoming more urgent, that is an \emph{upgrade} from the lower to the higher class --- whereas the solved model, Model-$B_2$, implements one-way jockeying in the \emph{opposite} direction, $1\to2$, a downgrade out of the priority queue. The readings are partly reconciled by interpreting the $1\to2$ transition as the events that genuinely move demand downward: administrative reclassification, clinical recovery or reprioritisation, or a triage correction demoting a patient who no longer meets the high-priority threshold. But the deterioration direction proper --- an upgrade $2\to1$, that is $\gamma_2>0$ --- is exactly the bidirectional case that Model-$B$ leaves open as a Volterra equation (Section~\ref{sec:model_b}). The mechanism the clinical motivation most wants is thus the one the analysis identifies as irreducibly hard, which sharpens rather than weakens the case for the future work below.

For a waiting-list manager the operational takeaways are concrete: monitor the second-tail mass $\mathbb{P}(N_1\ge2)$ --- the regime in which a congestion-control mechanism actually bites, and the proxy for the head-of-line gap of Section~\ref{sec:model_b21} --- track the loss fraction $P_{\mathrm{loss}}^{(1)}$ rather than the waiting time in isolation, and locate the operating point on the $(\rho,\theta_1)$ plane of the heatmap in Figure~\ref{fig:c2:heatmap}, whose favourable corner (abandonment most reduces the priority queue) is the high-load, high-$\theta_1$ region.

\subsection{Limitations}

The contribution is bounded by assumptions stated here proactively. Every result rests on exponential interarrival, service, jockeying, and abandonment times; relaxing any of these --- general service times in particular --- breaks the Markov structure on which the PGF method depends, as the $M/G/1$ intractability behind de~Waal's approximate analysis already illustrates. The treatment is single-server throughout, and it reports means rather than full waiting-time distributions. The partial-PGF result on the alternative state space is an \emph{approximation}, not a theorem (Approximation~\ref{apx:A:PPGF_Stilde}): it is reliable only in the priority-dominated, moderate-load region mapped in Figure~\ref{fig:val_ppgf} (Appendix~\ref{app:numerics:validation}), and unreliable elsewhere. The truncated-CTMC validation carries a small downward bias for the conserving (non-abandoning) models at $\rho\ge0.92$, already flagged in \S\ref{sec:comparison}; those near-critical points should be read as lower bounds. Finally, the class-2 waiting time reported for Model-$B_2$ is a per-entrant queue residence recovered through Little's law, not the end-to-end delay of a tagged customer followed across a jockeying transition --- a distinction that matters precisely because jockeying moves customers between the queues, and one the tagged-customer item below is meant to resolve.

\subsection{Future work}\label{sec:future_work}

Several extensions follow directly from the machinery already built, ranked here from the most immediate.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Head-of-line versions of the remaining models.} The head-of-line collapse that made Models~$\BH$ and~$\CH$ algebraic applies verbatim to the still-unsolved members of the hierarchy --- a head-of-line $B_1$ ($2\to1$ jockeying), $C$ and $C_1$ (class-2 and two-class abandonment), and even a head-of-line $X$ carrying \emph{both} mechanisms at the queue head. Each replaces a length-proportional rate by a constant $\mathbf 1_{\{n_1\ge1\}}$ rate, removes the derivative term, and is solvable by the Kernel Method exactly as in Sections~\ref{sec:model_b21}--\ref{sec:model_c21}. This is near-term low-hanging fruit that needs no new tools.
  \item \emph{The $k$-customer head-of-line ladder.} The head-of-line variants let only the single customer at the head of Queue~1 jockey or abandon; the natural next rung lets the first \emph{two} waiting class-1 customers do so, then the first three, and so on --- replacing the rate $\gamma_1\mathbf 1_{\{n_1\ge1\}}$ by $\gamma_1\min(n_1,k)$ for $k=2,3,\dots$ (and likewise $\theta_1\min(n_1,k)$). Writing $\min(n_1,k)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathbf 1_{\{n_1\ge j\}}$, the bounded rate keeps the fundamental equation \emph{algebraic} --- no derivative term appears --- at the price of $k$ unknown boundary functions $\widetilde P(n_1,y)$, $n_1=0,\dots,k-1$, in place of the single $P_y(y)$; whether the Kernel Method supplies enough pinning conditions for $k\ge2$ is precisely the open question. Since $\min(n_1,k)\uparrow n_1$, the ladder interpolates monotonically between $\BH$ (or $\CH$) at $k=1$ and the length-proportional Models~$B_2$ and~$C_2$ in the limit $k\to\infty$, so each solvable rung would yield a constructive approximation to the full-rate models --- a discrete counterpart to the parameter limits studied in Section~\ref{sec:results}, whose convergence in $k$ the CTMC pipeline of that section could quantify directly.
  \item \emph{Numerical inversion of the Model-$B$ Volterra equation.} Unlike Model-$X$, Model-$B$ yields a first-kind Volterra equation with an \emph{explicit} kernel and a \emph{known} forcing (Section~\ref{sec:model_b}); numerical quadrature of the boundary function is therefore immediately feasible and would deliver the class-2 distribution under bidirectional jockeying without a closed form.
  \item \emph{Boundary-value methods for Model-$B$.} A closed-form attack on the same equation belongs to the Riemann--Hilbert and boundary-value tradition of~\cite{fayolle1999random}, a programme that continues to yield explicit two-class results --- see the analytic-continuation analysis of Devos, Walraevens, Fiems \& Bruneel~\cite{devos2020alternating}; the straight-line characteristics of Model-$B$ make it the natural first test case for importing that machinery into the present framework.
  \item \emph{Tagged-customer sojourns.} The end-to-end delay of a class-2 customer in Model-$B_2$, followed across a possible jockeying transition, and the conditional time-to-service in Model-$C_2$ --- the latter already estimated by simulation as $\condWoneCtwo$ against the count-based $\uncondWoneCtwo$ --- both call for an analytic tagged-customer treatment that the mean-based analysis here does not provide.
  \item \emph{Waiting-time distributions.} The present means could be lifted to full sojourn-time distributions through a distributional Little's law, supplying the tail and quantile information that a mean cannot.
  \item \emph{Multi-server extension.} Carrying the mechanisms to $M/M/c$ would connect this exact single-server line to the approximate multi-server scheduling results of~\cite{huchandong2022proactive}, trading the exactness the single server buys for operational realism.
  \item \emph{Synchronised abandonment.} The independent patience clocks of Model-$C_2$ admit an orthogonal generalisation in which waiting customers abandon \emph{simultaneously}, each with probability $p$, at Poisson opportunity epochs. Kapodistria~\cite{kapodistria2011synchronized} solves the single-class case exactly through basic ($q$-)hypergeometric series with deformation parameter $q=1-p$, interpolating between the $M/M/1+M$ ($p\to0^+$, the Kummer regime of the class-1 subsystem here) and an $M/M/1$ with total catastrophes ($p\to1^-$). Whether the binomial bulk-departure structure survives the two-class non-preemptive setting --- in effect a $q$-deformation of the Model-$C_2$ analysis --- is open, and would add a synchronisation axis to the mechanism hierarchy of this thesis.
  \item \emph{Calibration.} Fitting $\gamma_1$, $\theta_1$ and the loads to real elderly-care waiting-list data~\cite{zenios1999modeling} would test whether the regimes identified here describe operational reality.
\end{enumerate}
A final, self-contained item is the closure of the partial-PGF recursion on the alternative state space $\widetilde{S}$, deferred from the analysis of Model-$A$ (Section~\ref{ssec:A:Stilde}). A rigorous treatment would retain the boundary-diagonal forcing that Approximation~\ref{apx:A:PPGF_Stilde} neglects and solve the resulting non-homogeneous recurrence by variation of constants in $n$ --- which, as anticipated there, reintroduces the unknown diagonal sequence $\{\pi(0,n)\}$ as the quantity to be determined self-consistently. Settling whether that recursion closes, and proving the conjectured monotonicity of $\mathbb{E}[N_2]$ in $\theta_1$ raised in Appendix~\ref{app:numerics:sweep}, would tie off the loose ends this thesis has deliberately left explicit.
