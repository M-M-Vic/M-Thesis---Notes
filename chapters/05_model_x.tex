\section{Analysis of Model-\texorpdfstring{$X$}{X} (\texorpdfstring{$\gamma_1,\gamma_2>0,\ \theta_1,\theta_2>0$}{gamma1,gamma2>0, theta1,theta2>0})}\label{sec:model_x_anal}

This section analyses the full model, Model-$X$, which retains \emph{both} bidirectional
jockeying ($\gamma_1,\gamma_2>0$) \emph{and} two-class abandonment
($\theta_1,\theta_2>0$), and from which every other model in this work is recovered by
zeroing parameters. No probabilistic route is available: jockeying breaks the per-class
Poisson/renewal structure---a class-2 customer cannot identify its effective service time
with a class-1 busy period---so the Pollaczek--Khinchine relation does not apply, while
abandonment, though it leaves each arrival stream Poisson, couples the two queues through
the state-dependent rates $\theta_i n_i$. The analysis therefore proceeds analytically.
The method of characteristics yields the homogeneous solution of the fundamental
equation; variation of parameters produces the general solution; the boundary behaviour
at $x=0$ pins the free function; and evaluating the result along the characteristic
through a diagonal point reduces the determination of the unknown boundary function
$P_y(y)$ to a Volterra integral equation of the first kind. There the analysis stops, for
two reasons established below: the kernel of that equation is built from a non-elementary
quadrature along transcendental characteristics, and its right-hand side is itself
coupled to an unknown diagonal gradient. Model-$X$ is therefore left open; its role in
the hierarchy is to expose the precise obstructions that its tractable specialisations
circumvent, and its queue layout is the left panel of
Figure~\ref{fig:models-queue-overview}.

\textbf{Stability.} Model-$X$ has abandonment from both classes, so the total departure
rate in state $(n_1,n_2)$ is $\mu+\theta_1 n_1+\theta_2 n_2$, which grows without bound
in every direction of the $(n_1,n_2)$-plane. This is made precise by a Foster--Lyapunov
argument with the linear test function $V(n_1,n_2)=n_1+n_2$: in any busy state the mean
one-step drift is $\mathbb{E}[\Delta V]=\lambda-\mu-\theta_1 n_1-\theta_2 n_2$ (arrivals
raise $V$ at rate $\lambda=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$, service and abandonment lower it at rate
$\mu+\theta_1 n_1+\theta_2 n_2$), which for any $\theta_1,\theta_2>0$ is bounded above by
$-\varepsilon<0$ for all $(n_1,n_2)$ outside the finite set $\{n_1+n_2\le(\lambda+\varepsilon)/\min(\theta_1,\theta_2)\}$;
by Foster's criterion~\cite{foster1953stochastic,meyn2009markov} the chain is therefore positive recurrent
for \emph{all} $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\mu>0$ and all $\theta_1,\theta_2>0$, with no
traffic-intensity restriction required (cf.\ the Erlang-A queue,
\S\ref{ssec:erlangA}). Setting $\theta_1=\theta_2=0$ removes this unbounded downward
drift, and the condition reverts to $\rho=(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)/\mu<1$ as in the
non-abandoning models (\S\S\ref{sec:model_a}--\ref{sec:model_b}).

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:X:pi00}
    The empty-system probability $\pi_0$ (server idle, no one waiting) and the
    both-queues-empty busy probability $\pi(0,0)$ (server busy, $N_1=N_2=0$) of
    Model-$X$ satisfy the universal idle balance
    \begin{equation}
        \pi(0,0) = \rho\,\pi_0,
        \qquad \rho=\frac{\lambda_1+\lambda_2}{\mu},
        \label{eq:X:pi00}
    \end{equation}
    as in every model of this work~\eqref{eq:gen:pi00_general}. With abandonment
    active, however, the total-customer process is a state-dependent
    birth--death chain rather than an $M/M/1$, so $\pi_0\neq 1-\rho$: the idle
    probability depends on $\theta_1,\theta_2$ and is fixed only by the global
    normalisation $P(1,1)=1-\pi_0$ once $P$ is known. Correspondingly, the diagonal of
    the PGF no longer collapses to $\pi(0,0)/(1-\rho z)$; setting $x=y=z$ in the
    fundamental equation leaves the abandonment-corrected relation
    \begin{equation}
        (\mu-\lambda z)\,P(z,z)
        + z\bigl[\theta_1\,\partial_x P + \theta_2\,\partial_y P\bigr]_{(z,z)}
        = \mu\,\pi(0,0),
        \qquad \lambda=\lambda_1+\lambda_2.
        \label{eq:X:diagonal}
    \end{equation}
    As $\theta_1,\theta_2\to 0^+$ the gradient terms drop out and~\eqref{eq:X:diagonal}
    returns the identities $P(z,z)=\pi(0,0)/(1-\rho z)$, $\pi_0=1-\rho$ and
    $\pi(0,0)=\rho(1-\rho)$ of the non-abandoning
    models~\eqref{eq:B:pi00},~\eqref{eq:A:pi0_pi00}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Set $x=y=z$ in the fundamental equation~\eqref{eq:gen:fundamental_equation} and
    track its four groups of terms separately. The two jockeying contributions carry the
    factor $(x-y)$ and vanish, as does the boundary term $\mu(x-y)P_y(y)$; the
    abandonment contributions, carrying instead the factors $(x-1)$ and $(y-1)$,
    survive:
    \begin{align}
        xy\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\,\partial_x P\,\Big|_{x=y=z}
        &= z^2\,\theta_1(z-1)\,\partial_x P(z,z),
        \nonumber\\
        xy\bigl[\gamma_2(y-x)+\theta_2(y-1)\bigr]\,\partial_y P\,\Big|_{x=y=z}
        &= z^2\,\theta_2(z-1)\,\partial_y P(z,z).
        \nonumber
    \end{align}
    The algebraic coefficient of $P$ factorises, with $\lambda=\lambda_1+\lambda_2$,
    \begin{align}
        \begin{split}
            (\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\mu)z^2-\mu z-\lambda_1 z^3-\lambda_2 z^3
            &= z\bigl[-\lambda z^2+(\lambda+\mu)z-\mu\bigr] \\
            &= -z\,(\lambda z-\mu)(z-1)
            \;=\; z(z-1)(\mu-\lambda z),
        \end{split}
        \nonumber
    \end{align}
    and the surviving right-hand side is $-\mu z(1-z)\pi(0,0)=\mu z(z-1)\pi(0,0)$.
    Collecting,
    \begin{align}
        z(z-1)(\mu-\lambda z)P(z,z)
        + z^2(z-1)\bigl[\theta_1\,\partial_x P + \theta_2\,\partial_y P\bigr]_{(z,z)}
        = \mu z(z-1)\,\pi(0,0),
        \label{eq:X:diagonal_full}
    \end{align}
    and dividing by the common factor $z(z-1)\neq0$ (for $z\in(0,1)$)
    gives~\eqref{eq:X:diagonal}. The idle state $(0)$ exchanges probability flux only
    with the both-queues-empty busy state, so global balance across the idle--busy cut
    reads $\lambda\pi_0=\mu\pi(0,0)$~\eqref{eq:gen:balance_idle} (an arrival at total rate
    $\lambda$ leaves $(0)$; a service completion at rate $\mu$ returns to it), whence
    $\pi(0,0)=\rho\pi_0$, which is~\eqref{eq:X:pi00}. When $\theta_1=\theta_2=0$ the
    gradient terms in~\eqref{eq:X:diagonal} are absent, so
    $P(z,z)=\pi(0,0)/(1-\rho z)$; evaluating at $z=1$ with $P(1,1)=1-\pi_0$ then forces
    $(1-\pi_0)(1-\rho)=\rho\pi_0$, i.e.\ $\pi_0=1-\rho$. With abandonment those gradient
    terms are present and \emph{not} known, so the diagonal no longer determines
    $P(z,z)$, and $\pi_0$ cannot be read off in closed form: it follows only from the
    full solution via normalisation. The same gradient coupling resurfaces in Step~5 of
    the analysis below, and again, in scalar form, for Model-$C_2$
    (\S\ref{sec:model_c2}).
\end{proof}

\begin{claim}\label{clm:X:volterra}
    Under positive recurrence, the bivariate PGF of Model-$X$ satisfies, on each
    characteristic curve $\Phi(x,y)=C_1$ of the fundamental equation, the integral
    representation
    \begin{equation}
        P(x,y(x)) \;=\; P_h(x,y(x))\int_{0}^{x}
        \frac{h\bigl(t;\,C_1\bigr)}{P_h\bigl(t,\,y(t)\bigr)}\,dt,
        \label{eq:X:integral_rep}
    \end{equation}
    where $P_h$ is the homogeneous solution~\eqref{eq:X:Ph}, $y(t)=y(t;C_1)$ is the
    characteristic parametrisation, $\Phi$ is the first integral~\eqref{eq:X:first_integral}
    (a transcendental function of $(x,y)$), and
    $h(t;C_1)$ is a forcing function depending on the unknown boundary trace
    $P_y(y)=P(0,y)$. Evaluating~\eqref{eq:X:integral_rep} along the characteristic
    through a diagonal point $(z,z)$ reduces the problem to a \emph{Volterra integral
    equation of the first kind} for $P_y$:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathcal{F}(z) \;=\; \int_0^z \mathcal{K}(z,t)\,P_y\!\bigl(y(t;z)\bigr)\,dt,
        \label{eq:X:volterra}
    \end{equation}
    whose kernel $\mathcal{K}$ and right-hand side $\mathcal{F}$ are defined
    in~\eqref{eq:X:kernel}--\eqref{eq:X:rhs} below. The kernel $\mathcal{K}$ is built
    from the non-elementary homogeneous solution $P_h$ and does not simplify for general
    $(\gamma_1,\gamma_2,\theta_1,\theta_2)$; moreover, the
    right-hand side $\mathcal{F}$ is \emph{not} fully known~---~it is coupled
    through~\eqref{eq:X:diagonal} to the diagonal gradient
    $[\theta_1\partial_x P+\theta_2\partial_y P]_{(z,z)}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:X:volterra}
    therefore does not reduce to a closed-form solution for $P_y$, and Model-$X$ is left
    open.
\end{claim}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Claim~\ref{clm:X:volterra}]
The proof proceeds in five steps: (i)~homogeneous solution via the method of
characteristics, (ii)~behaviour of $P_h$ at the boundaries, (iii)~particular solution
via variation of parameters, (iv)~pinning the free function from the boundary condition,
and (v)~diagonal evaluation yielding the Volterra equation.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Step~1: Homogeneous solution via the method of characteristics.}

The fundamental equation~\eqref{eq:gen:fundamental_equation} is a first-order linear PDE
in $P(x,y)$. Every one of its coefficients contains the overall factor $y$: the
algebraic coefficient factorises as
\begin{equation}
    (\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\mu)xy - \mu y - \lambda_1 x^2 y - \lambda_2 x y^2
    \;=\; y\,A(x,y),
    \qquad
    A(x,y) := (\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\mu)x - \mu - \lambda_1 x^2 - \lambda_2 xy,
    \label{eq:X:Adef}
\end{equation}
and both convection coefficients carry the prefactor $xy$. Setting the right-hand side
to zero and dividing through by $y$ therefore yields the homogeneous PDE
\begin{equation}
    x\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}
    + x\bigl[\gamma_2(y-x)+\theta_2(y-1)\bigr]\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}
    = -A(x,y)\,P.
    \label{eq:X:homogeneous_pde}
\end{equation}
Following the method of characteristics
(\S\ref{sssec:moc}), we associate to~\eqref{eq:X:homogeneous_pde} the characteristic
system
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dx}{x\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]}
    = \frac{dy}{x\bigl[\gamma_2(y-x)+\theta_2(y-1)\bigr]}
    = \frac{dP}{-A(x,y)\,P}.
    \label{eq:X:char_system}
\end{equation}
Taking the ratio of the first two differentials, the common factor $x$ cancels:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dy}{dx}
    = \frac{\gamma_2(y-x)+\theta_2(y-1)}{\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)}
    = \frac{-\gamma_2 x + (\gamma_2+\theta_2)y - \theta_2}
           {(\gamma_1+\theta_1)x - \gamma_1 y - \theta_1}.
    \label{eq:X:char_ode}
\end{equation}
When $\theta_1=\theta_2=0$ the two affine forms in~\eqref{eq:X:char_ode} share the
factor $(x-y)$, the slope collapses to the constant $-\gamma_2/\gamma_1$, and the
characteristics are the straight lines $\gamma_2 x+\gamma_1 y=\mathrm{const}$ (the
jockeying-only case of \S\ref{sec:model_b}). With abandonment active no common factor
survives: \eqref{eq:X:char_ode} is a genuine first-order ODE whose right-hand side is a
ratio of two affine forms, and its integral curves are not straight lines.

Both affine forms in~\eqref{eq:X:char_ode} vanish simultaneously only at the single
point $(x,y)=(1,1)$ (the unique intersection of the lines
$(\gamma_1+\theta_1)x-\gamma_1 y-\theta_1=0$ and
$-\gamma_2 x+(\gamma_2+\theta_2)y-\theta_2=0$), which is therefore the lone singular
point of the characteristic field. Shifting to it via $X=x-1,\ Y=y-1$ removes the
constant terms and renders~\eqref{eq:X:char_ode} homogeneous:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dY}{dX}
    = \frac{-\gamma_2 X + (\gamma_2+\theta_2)Y}{(\gamma_1+\theta_1)X - \gamma_1 Y}.
    \label{eq:X:char_shifted}
\end{equation}
The right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:X:char_shifted} depends on $(X,Y)$ only through the
ratio $v:=Y/X$, so we substitute $Y=vX$, $\frac{dY}{dX}=v+X\frac{dv}{dX}$, and isolate
$X\,dv/dX$ over the common denominator:
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        v + X\frac{dv}{dX}
        &= \frac{-\gamma_2 + (\gamma_2+\theta_2)\,v}{(\gamma_1+\theta_1) - \gamma_1 v}
    \end{split}
    \nonumber\\[2pt]
    \begin{split}
        \Longrightarrow\qquad
        X\frac{dv}{dX}
        &= \frac{\bigl[-\gamma_2+(\gamma_2+\theta_2)v\bigr]
                 - v\bigl[(\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v\bigr]}
                {(\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v}
        = \frac{\gamma_1 v^2+(\gamma_2+\theta_2-\gamma_1-\theta_1)v-\gamma_2}
                {(\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v}.
    \end{split}
    \nonumber
\end{align}
Naming the quadratic in the numerator $Q(v)$, the variables separate:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{(\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v}{Q(v)}\,dv = \frac{dX}{X},
    \qquad
    Q(v) := \gamma_1 v^2 + (\gamma_2+\theta_2-\gamma_1-\theta_1)\,v - \gamma_2 .
    \label{eq:X:char_separated}
\end{equation}
The quadratic $Q$ has discriminant
$D=(\gamma_2+\theta_2-\gamma_1-\theta_1)^2+4\gamma_1\gamma_2>0$ and real roots of opposite
sign (their product is $-\gamma_2/\gamma_1<0$),
\begin{equation}
    v_{\pm}=\frac{-(\gamma_2+\theta_2-\gamma_1-\theta_1)\pm\sqrt{D}}{2\gamma_1},
    \qquad v_-<0<v_+ .
    \label{eq:X:roots}
\end{equation}
Decompose the left-hand side of~\eqref{eq:X:char_separated} by partial fractions over
$Q(v)=\gamma_1(v-v_+)(v-v_-)$: writing
\begin{equation}
    \frac{(\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v}{\gamma_1(v-v_+)(v-v_-)}
    = \frac{a_+}{v-v_+} + \frac{a_-}{v-v_-}
    \label{eq:X:pf_ansatz}
\end{equation}
and clearing denominators,
\begin{equation}
    (\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v
    \;=\; \gamma_1\bigl[\,a_+(v-v_-)+a_-(v-v_+)\,\bigr].
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
Evaluating at $v=v_+$ and $v=v_-$ in turn isolates each coefficient, while comparing
the coefficients of $v$ on the two sides gives $-\gamma_1=\gamma_1(a_++a_-)$:
\begin{equation}
    a_{\pm}=\frac{(\gamma_1+\theta_1)-\gamma_1 v_{\pm}}{\gamma_1\,(v_{\pm}-v_{\mp})},
    \qquad a_++a_-=-1
    \label{eq:X:apm}
\end{equation}
(equivalently, $a_++a_-$ is read off from the $1/v$ decay of the integrand at
$v=\infty$). Integrating~\eqref{eq:X:char_separated} term by term and exponentiating,
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        a_+\ln(v-v_+)+a_-\ln(v-v_-) &= \ln X+\mathrm{const}
    \end{split}
    \nonumber\\[2pt]
    \begin{split}
        \Longrightarrow\qquad
        (v-v_+)^{a_+}\,(v-v_-)^{a_-} &= C\,X .
    \end{split}
    \nonumber
\end{align}
Returning to $(X,Y)$ via $v-v_\pm=(Y-v_\pm X)/X$, the powers of $X$ collect into a
single factor that cancels against the right-hand side:
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        (v-v_+)^{a_+}(v-v_-)^{a_-}
        &= \bigl(Y-v_+X\bigr)^{a_+}\bigl(Y-v_-X\bigr)^{a_-}\,X^{-(a_++a_-)} \\
        &= \bigl(Y-v_+X\bigr)^{a_+}\bigl(Y-v_-X\bigr)^{a_-}\,X
        \qquad\text{(by } a_++a_-=-1\text{)},
    \end{split}
    \nonumber
\end{align}
so $\bigl(Y-v_+X\bigr)^{a_+}\bigl(Y-v_-X\bigr)^{a_-}=C$. With $X=x-1$, $Y=y-1$ we
obtain the first integral
\begin{equation}
    \Phi(x,y)
    := \bigl[(y-1)-v_+(x-1)\bigr]^{a_+}\,\bigl[(y-1)-v_-(x-1)\bigr]^{a_-}
    = C_1,
    \label{eq:X:first_integral}
\end{equation}
constant along each characteristic. The first integral~\eqref{eq:X:first_integral} is a
product of non-integer powers of two linear forms~---~a transcendental level curve, not
a linear one. As a consistency check, let $\theta_1,\theta_2\to0^+$: the quadratic
factorises,
\begin{equation}
    Q(v)\;\to\;\gamma_1 v^2+(\gamma_2-\gamma_1)v-\gamma_2
    =(\gamma_1 v+\gamma_2)(v-1),
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
so $v_+\to1$ and $v_-\to-\gamma_2/\gamma_1$, and the closed forms~\eqref{eq:X:apm} give
\begin{equation}
    a_+\;\to\;\frac{\gamma_1-\gamma_1\cdot 1}{\gamma_1(v_+-v_-)}=0,
    \qquad
    a_-\;\to\;\frac{\gamma_1-\gamma_1 v_-}{\gamma_1(v_--v_+)}
    =\frac{1-v_-}{v_--1}=-1 .
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
The $v_+$ factor of $\Phi$ then carries exponent $0$ and drops out, leaving
$\Phi(x,y)\to\bigl[(y-1)+(\gamma_2/\gamma_1)(x-1)\bigr]^{-1}$, whose level curves are
exactly the straight lines $\gamma_2 x+\gamma_1 y=\mathrm{const}$ of the jockeying-only
case.

To find $P$ along a characteristic we take the ratio of the first and third differentials
in~\eqref{eq:X:char_system},
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dP}{P}
    = \frac{-A\bigl(x,y(x)\bigr)}
           {x\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y(x))+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]}\,dx ,
    \label{eq:X:dPP}
\end{equation}
where $y(x)=y(x;C_1)$ is the characteristic~\eqref{eq:X:first_integral}. Integrating
yields the general homogeneous solution
\begin{equation}
    P_h(x,y)
    = F(C_1)\,\exp\!\left(
        -\int_{x_\star}^{x}
        \frac{A\bigl(s,y(s)\bigr)}
             {s\bigl[\gamma_1(s-y(s))+\theta_1(s-1)\bigr]}\,ds
    \right),
    \qquad C_1=\Phi(x,y),
    \label{eq:X:Ph}
\end{equation}
where $F$ is an arbitrary differentiable function of the characteristic constant and
$x_\star$ is an arbitrary base point (its choice is absorbed into $F$). Here the
closed-form algebra ends: because $y(s)$ is defined only implicitly by the
transcendental level set~\eqref{eq:X:first_integral}, the integrand
in~\eqref{eq:X:Ph} is not a rational function of $s$, and the quadrature is not
elementary. No explicit closed form for $P_h$ exists.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Step~2: Boundary behaviour of $P_h$.}

Although the quadrature~\eqref{eq:X:Ph} is not elementary, the two qualitative features
needed downstream can be established directly from the local behaviour of its
integrand.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(a) Blow-up at $x=0$.}
A characteristic meets the axis $x=0$ at a finite height $y_0:=y(0;C_1)\in(0,1]$.
Evaluating the two ingredients of the exponent integrand there: from~\eqref{eq:X:Adef},
$A(0,y_0)=-\mu$, and the convection bracket is
$\gamma_1(0-y_0)+\theta_1(0-1)=-(\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1)<0$. Hence, as $s\to0^+$,
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{A\bigl(s,y(s)\bigr)}{s\bigl[\gamma_1(s-y(s))+\theta_1(s-1)\bigr]}
    \;\sim\;
    -\frac{-\mu}{s\cdot\bigl[-(\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1)\bigr]}
    \;=\;
    -\frac{\mu}{(\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1)\,s}\,,
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
a simple pole whose integral contributes $-\mu/(\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1)\cdot\ln x$ to
the exponent in~\eqref{eq:X:Ph}, so
\begin{equation}
    P_h\bigl(x,y(x)\bigr)\ \sim\ x^{-\mu/(\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1)}\ \longrightarrow\ +\infty
    \qquad (x\to0^+),
    \label{eq:X:Ph_at_zero}
\end{equation}
with a strictly negative exponent because $\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1>0$. This blow-up
drives the pinning of $F$ in Step~4.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{(b) Regularity on the diagonal.}
On the diagonal $x=y=z$ the $x$-convection coefficient reduces to
\begin{equation}
    x\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\Big|_{x=y=z}
    \;=\; z\,\theta_1(z-1)\;\neq\;0
    \qquad\text{for } z\in(0,1):
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
the abandonment term $\theta_1(x-1)$ keeps the characteristic field away from zero
there, even though the jockeying part $\gamma_1(x-y)$ vanishes. Consequently the
integrand in~\eqref{eq:X:Ph} is regular as $x\to z$ along the diagonal-crossing
characteristic, and
\begin{equation}
    P_h^{\,\mathrm{diag}}(z):=\lim_{x\to z}P_h\bigl(x,y(x;z)\bigr)
    \quad\text{is finite and non-zero.}
    \label{eq:X:Ph_diag}
\end{equation}
Were $\theta_1$ zero, the coefficient would vanish on the diagonal and equip $P_h$ with
an interior singularity there~---~the singularity that dominates the jockeying-only
analyses (\S\S\ref{sec:model_b}--\ref{sec:model_b2}). With abandonment present no such
singularity arises; as Step~5 shows, the obstruction migrates instead to the right-hand
side of the Volterra equation.

\smallskip\noindent\textit{Branch convention.}
The non-integer powers in~\eqref{eq:X:first_integral} and the quadrature
in~\eqref{eq:X:Ph} may carry a complex phase off the real region; this phase is absorbed
into the arbitrary function $F(C_1)$ and cancels exactly in the ratio
$P_h(x,y(x))/P_h(t,y(t))$ appearing in~\eqref{eq:X:integral_rep}, since numerator and
denominator lie on the same characteristic (same $C_1$).

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Step~3: Particular solution via variation of parameters.}

We now restrict the full (inhomogeneous) fundamental
equation~\eqref{eq:gen:fundamental_equation} to a fixed characteristic $y=y(x;C_1)$.
Along it the total derivative of $P$ is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d}{dx}P\bigl(x,y(x)\bigr)=\partial_x P + y'(x)\,\partial_y P,
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
and the characteristic ODE~\eqref{eq:X:char_ode}, cleared of its denominator, states
precisely that
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_2(y-x)+\theta_2(y-1)
    = \bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\,y'(x).
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
Substituting this for the second convection bracket, the two convection terms combine
into a single total derivative:
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        &xy\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\partial_x P
        + xy\bigl[\gamma_2(y-x)+\theta_2(y-1)\bigr]\partial_y P \\
        &\qquad
        = xy\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]
          \bigl(\partial_x P + y'(x)\,\partial_y P\bigr)
        = xy\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\frac{d}{dx}P\bigl(x,y(x)\bigr).
    \end{split}
    \nonumber
\end{align}
The fundamental equation along the characteristic therefore reads, using the
factorisation~\eqref{eq:X:Adef} of its algebraic coefficient,
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        y\,A(x,y)\,P
        + xy\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]\frac{dP}{dx}
        \;=\; G(x,y),
    \end{split}
    \nonumber
\end{align}
and dividing through by $xy\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y)+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]$ reduces the PDE to
the first-order linear ODE (for fixed $C_1$)
\begin{equation}
    \frac{dP}{dx} = f(x;C_1)\,P + h(x;C_1),
    \label{eq:X:ode_char}
\end{equation}
with coefficient and forcing
\begin{align}
    f(x;C_1) &= -\frac{A\bigl(x,y(x)\bigr)}
                       {x\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y(x))+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]},
    \label{eq:X:fdef}\\[4pt]
    h(x;C_1) &= \frac{G\bigl(x,y(x)\bigr)}
                     {x\,y(x)\bigl[\gamma_1(x-y(x))+\theta_1(x-1)\bigr]},
    \label{eq:X:hdef}
\end{align}
where $A$ is as in~\eqref{eq:X:Adef} and $G$ denotes the right-hand side of the
fundamental equation,
\begin{equation}
    G(x,y) := \mu(x-y)\,P_y(y) - \mu x(1-y)\,\pi(0,0).
    \label{eq:X:Gdef}
\end{equation}
Note that the denominator of both~\eqref{eq:X:fdef} and~\eqref{eq:X:hdef} is the
$x$-convection coefficient itself: every analytic feature of the problem established in
Step~2~---~the blow-up at $x=0$, the regularity on the diagonal~---~traces back to its
zeros. Since $P_h$ from~\eqref{eq:X:Ph} satisfies the homogeneous equation
$\frac{d}{dx}P_h=f(x;C_1)P_h$, it is the fundamental solution
of~\eqref{eq:X:ode_char}; applying variation of parameters (\S\ref{sssec:vop}), the
general solution of the inhomogeneous ODE is
\begin{equation}
    P(x,y(x)) = P_h(x,y(x))\!\left[F(C_1)
        + \int_{x_0}^{x} \frac{h(t;C_1)}{P_h(t,y(t))}\,dt\right].
    \label{eq:X:variation}
\end{equation}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Step~4: Pinning the free function from the boundary condition at $x=0$.}

Along any characteristic the endpoint at $x=0$ is $y(0)=y_0$, giving
$P(0,y_0)=P_y(y_0)$, which is finite. However, by~\eqref{eq:X:Ph_at_zero} the homogeneous
factor blows up, $P_h(x,y(x))\to+\infty$ as $x\to0^+$, while the integral
in~\eqref{eq:X:variation} vanishes as $x_0\to0$ (the integration interval collapses).
Therefore
\[
    \lim_{x\to0^+}P(x,y(x))
    = \lim_{x\to0^+}P_h(x,y(x))\cdot F(C_1)
    = +\infty\cdot F(C_1),
\]
which can be finite only if $F(C_1)=0$. Setting $x_0=0$ and $F(C_1)=0$
in~\eqref{eq:X:variation} gives the integral representation of
Claim~\ref{clm:X:volterra},
\begin{equation}
    P(x,y(x)) = P_h(x,y(x))\int_{0}^{x} \frac{h(t;C_1)}{P_h(t,y(t))}\,dt.
    \label{eq:X:integral_rep_body}
\end{equation}
The blow-up exponent $\mu/(\gamma_1 y_0+\theta_1)$ of~\eqref{eq:X:Ph_at_zero} is
strictly positive on every characteristic, so the conclusion $F\equiv0$ holds for the
entire family.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Step~5: Reduction to the Volterra equation.}

We simplify the forcing $h$ on the characteristic. Substituting $G$
from~\eqref{eq:X:Gdef} into~\eqref{eq:X:hdef}, then splitting the fraction and
cancelling one factor of $t$ in the second term,
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        h(t;C_1)
        &= \frac{\mu\,(t-y(t))\,P_y\!\bigl(y(t)\bigr)
                 \;-\;\mu\,t\,(1-y(t))\,\pi(0,0)}
                {t\,y(t)\bigl[\gamma_1(t-y(t))+\theta_1(t-1)\bigr]} \\[4pt]
        &= \frac{\mu\,(t-y(t))}{t\,y(t)\bigl[\gamma_1(t-y(t))+\theta_1(t-1)\bigr]}\,P_y\!\bigl(y(t)\bigr)
        - \frac{\mu\,(1-y(t))}{y(t)\bigl[\gamma_1(t-y(t))+\theta_1(t-1)\bigr]}\,\pi(0,0).
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:X:h_simplified}
\end{align}
We evaluate~\eqref{eq:X:integral_rep_body} along the characteristic through a diagonal
point $(z,z)$, that is, the curve with $C_1=\Phi(z,z)$; its parametrisation $y(t;z)$
satisfies $y(z;z)=z$. Setting $x=z$,
\begin{equation}
    P(z,z) = P_h(z,z)\int_0^z \frac{h\bigl(t;\Phi(z,z)\bigr)}{P_h\bigl(t,y(t;z)\bigr)}\,dt.
    \label{eq:X:diag_eval}
\end{equation}
By the regularity~\eqref{eq:X:Ph_diag}, $P_h(z,z)=P_h^{\,\mathrm{diag}}(z)$ is finite and
non-zero and the integrand is regular as $t\to z$ (its numerator
$t-y(t;z)\to0$), so~\eqref{eq:X:diag_eval} is a \emph{proper} integral and no
finite-part regularisation is needed. Substituting the two
pieces of~\eqref{eq:X:h_simplified} and separating the term containing the unknown $P_y$
from the known remainder, define the kernel and remainder
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{K}(z,t) &:= \frac{\mu\,\bigl(t-y(t;z)\bigr)\,P_h^{\,\mathrm{diag}}(z)}
        {t\,y(t;z)\bigl[\gamma_1(t-y(t;z))+\theta_1(t-1)\bigr]\,P_h\bigl(t,y(t;z)\bigr)},
    \label{eq:X:kernel}\\[6pt]
    \mathcal{R}(z) &:= -\,\mu\,\pi(0,0)\,P_h^{\,\mathrm{diag}}(z)
        \int_0^z \frac{1-y(t;z)}
        {y(t;z)\bigl[\gamma_1(t-y(t;z))+\theta_1(t-1)\bigr]\,P_h\bigl(t,y(t;z)\bigr)}\,dt,
    \label{eq:X:Rdef}
\end{align}
so that~\eqref{eq:X:diag_eval} reads
$P(z,z)=\int_0^z\mathcal{K}(z,t)\,P_y(y(t;z))\,dt+\mathcal{R}(z)$. It remains to
express the left-hand side $P(z,z)$. Solving the diagonal
relation~\eqref{eq:X:diagonal} for $P(z,z)$ and using
$\mu/(\mu-\lambda z)=1/(1-\rho z)$,
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        (\mu-\lambda z)\,P(z,z)
        &= \mu\,\pi(0,0)
        - z\bigl[\theta_1\partial_x P+\theta_2\partial_y P\bigr]_{(z,z)} \\
        \Longrightarrow\qquad
        P(z,z)
        &= \frac{\pi(0,0)}{1-\rho z}
        - \frac{z}{\mu-\lambda z}
          \bigl[\theta_1\partial_x P+\theta_2\partial_y P\bigr]_{(z,z)} .
    \end{split}
    \label{eq:X:diag_solved}
\end{align}
Setting
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}(z) := P(z,z) - \mathcal{R}(z)
    = \underbrace{\frac{\pi(0,0)}{1-\rho z}}_{\theta_1=\theta_2=0\ \text{value}}
      \;-\;\underbrace{\frac{z}{\mu-\lambda z}
        \bigl[\theta_1\partial_x P + \theta_2\partial_y P\bigr]_{(z,z)}}_{\text{abandonment coupling}}
      \;-\;\mathcal{R}(z),
    \label{eq:X:rhs}
\end{equation}
equation~\eqref{eq:X:diag_eval} becomes the Volterra
integral equation of the first kind~\eqref{eq:X:volterra}, confirming
Claim~\ref{clm:X:volterra}.
Two features distinguish~\eqref{eq:X:volterra} from its Model-$B$ analogue, and both make
Model-$X$ strictly less tractable. First, the kernel~\eqref{eq:X:kernel} is built from the
non-elementary homogeneous solution $P_h$ along transcendental characteristics, so it has
no closed form for general parameters (in Model-$B$, $P_h$ and the straight-line
characteristics were explicit). Second, and decisively, the right-hand
side~\eqref{eq:X:rhs} is \emph{not} known data: through~\eqref{eq:X:diagonal} it is
coupled to the diagonal gradient $[\theta_1\partial_x P+\theta_2\partial_y P]_{(z,z)}$,
which is precisely the quantity that abandonment prevents the diagonal from
determining~\eqref{eq:C2:diag}. Where Model-$B$ left a clean first-kind Volterra equation
with explicit kernel and known forcing (itself already beyond closed-form inversion),
Model-$X$ leaves one whose kernel is implicit and whose forcing is entangled with the
unknown solution. Resolving it lies well beyond the scope of this thesis.
\end{proof}

Model-$X$ is therefore left open at this point. Its role in the hierarchy is to make the
two obstructions explicit and to show how each tractable specialisation removes them. The
limit $\theta_1=\theta_2\to0$ (Model-$B$) restores both at once: the
characteristics~\eqref{eq:X:first_integral} straighten to the lines
$\gamma_2 x+\gamma_1 y=\mathrm{const}$, so the kernel~\eqref{eq:X:kernel} becomes
explicit, and the gradient coupling in~\eqref{eq:X:rhs} vanishes, so
$\mathcal{F}(z)=\pi(0,0)/(1-\rho z)-\mathcal{R}(z)$ becomes known data~---~leaving the
clean (still unsolved) Volterra equation of \S\ref{sec:model_b}. Removing one
mechanism at a time then closes the problem entirely: setting in addition $\gamma_2=0$
(Model-$B_2$) collapses the characteristic family to vertical lines and degenerates the
PDE to an ODE in $x$ alone, whose boundary function $P_y(y)$ is pinned by an analyticity
argument at the interior singular point $x=y$ (\S\ref{sec:model_b2}); removing
jockeying entirely while keeping only class-1 abandonment (Model-$C_2$) likewise
degenerates the PDE to an ODE in $x$, whose $P_y(y)$ is pinned by a boundedness argument
at $x=1$ (\S\ref{sec:model_c2}); and zeroing all four parameters returns the fully
solvable baseline Model-$A$ (\S\ref{sec:model_a}). The full model~$X$, retaining
all mechanisms, retains both obstructions simultaneously and is the one case that does
not close.
